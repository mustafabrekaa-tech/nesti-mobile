## 3 · Register — the open question

I wrote everything in formal MSA on the assumption that an institutional
buyer expects it. That may be the wrong call for Egyptian private schools,
where the owner and the principal often talk in educated colloquial and
formal MSA can read as distant or as advertising boilerplate.

Three options:

1. **Formal MSA throughout** — what I did. Safe for Gulf, possibly stiff for
   Cairo.
2. **Lighter MSA** — same vocabulary, shorter sentences, no literary
   constructions. Reads as a professional email rather than a press release.
3. **Educated Egyptian** — the way an operations manager actually speaks in a
   meeting. Warmest, best for Egypt, weaker for Saudi.

My instinct now is **2 for Egypt and the Gulf together**, but this is your
market and your ear, not mine.

## 4 · A structural fix, not just vocabulary

The deeper problem is that I wrote English first and translated. Even with
perfect word choices that produces Arabic with English sentence rhythm.

From here I'll write the Arabic first, as the primary text, and let the
English follow it. That changes the sentences, not only the words.
