# NESTI — School-facing content (B2B)

Audience: owners, principals (مدير/مديرة المدرسة), operations and admin managers
of Egyptian and Gulf private schools. Not parents.

Register: Modern Standard Arabic, professional but not stiff. No Egyptian
colloquial — it reads as consumer marketing to an institutional buyer.
English is a parallel construction, not a translation.

Rule applied throughout: schools do not buy "communication". They buy fewer
complaints, faster collection, defensible records, and an edge at enrollment.
Every piece below leads with a business problem, not a product feature.

---

**EN**

# LinkedIn — long form

## A · "The complaint that costs more than it looks"

**EN**

Most school complaints are about something that went wrong. Those are
manageable — something broke, you fix it, the parent moves on.

The expensive one is different. It is the parent who believes they were never
told.

Nothing broke. The trip notice went out. The fee reminder was sent. The
schedule changed and somebody did tell the class. But it travelled by group
chat, or by a page in a schoolbag, and now there is no way to show what
happened. The conversation becomes one person's memory against another's.

Schools lose standing in that conversation even when they did everything
right, because being right and being able to demonstrate it are not the same
thing.

This is the gap NESTI was built around. Every notice has a time, a recipient
and a record. Not to win arguments with parents — to make most of them
unnecessary.

We are working with schools in Egypt and the Gulf for Term 2. If this is a
conversation you have had more than once this year, it is worth a call.

**AR**

وينتهي الأمر.

الشكوى المكلفة نوع آخر: وليّ أمر مقتنع بأن أحدًا لم يُبلغه.

لم يقع خطأ. إشعار الرحلة أُرسل، والتذكير بالرسوم أُرسل، والجدول تغيّر وأبلغ

## B · "Six systems and none of them talk"

**EN**

Ask a school operations manager where attendance lives, and you will usually
get a different answer than you get for fees, or buses, or health notes. Each
is in the right place on its own. Together they are six places, and nobody
owns the join.

That is not a technology failure. It is how school administration grew — one
problem solved at a time, each with whatever tool was to hand that year.

The cost only shows up at the edges. A parent asks a question that spans two
systems. An inspection asks for a record that lives in a drawer. A new admin
takes three months to become useful because the knowledge was never written
down anywhere.

NESTI puts the school day in one place: routes, attendance, homework, health
notes, fees, and the communication around all of it. Not a new way to run a
school. The same way, in one system that can answer questions.

Term 2 onboarding runs through December and January. If you want the term to
start clean, now is when that gets decided.

مكانه الصحيح منفردًا. ومجتمعةً هي ستة أماكن، ولا أحد يملك الربط بينها.

هذا ليس فشلًا تقنيًا. هكذا نمت الإدارة المدرسية: مشكلة تُحَل في كل مرة، بما كان

والتكلفة لا تظهر إلا عند الحواف. وليّ أمر يسأل سؤالًا يمتد عبر نظامين. جهة

# What this deliberately avoids

- **No fear.** No missing-child imagery, no implied negligence. Principals are
  the people most tired of that register, and it reads as a threat to their
  competence.
- **No invented numbers.** No "schools save 40%". Nothing is claimed that
  cannot be shown in a demo.
- **No colloquial Arabic.** The parent campaign used Egyptian colloquial
  correctly. Using it here would read as consumer marketing to an
  institutional buyer.
- **No feature lists.** Every piece opens on a business problem the reader has
  already had, and reaches the product second or not at all.
- **No parent-emotional framing.** "You'll feel calm" is a parent benefit.
  Schools buy evidence, time and standing.
